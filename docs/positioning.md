## The core positioning

> We help pastors and executive directors get back to leading.

This is not a slogan invented for the site. It is the feedback Lana receives
consistently from the pastors and CEOs she works with: her presence frees them
to do the job they were called to do.

Underneath it sits the working definition of the expertise:

> Diagnose the gap between what an organization says it is and how it actually
> operates, then rebuild the infrastructure underneath until the two match.

## What makes it specific rather than generic

1. **Diagnostic-first as a discipline, not a phase.** A ninety-day assessment
   before any prescription. Built into a sellable methodology rather than kept
   as a personal habit.
2. **Capacity as structural, not tactical.** Diagnosing donor concentration as
   a structural fragility and insisting on a feasibility study before a capital
   campaign is capacity building, not fundraising.
3. **The unglamorous infrastructure layer.** Auto-revoked 501(c)(3)
   reinstatements, payroll, HR files, state filings. Most capacity consultants
   will not touch this. It is what actually breaks small organizations.
4. **Physical and technical systems as capacity constraints.** Building systems,
   HVAC, network rebuilds. Almost nobody in this field reads a building as part
   of organizational capacity.
5. **Leadership culture with the power analysis intact.** Goleman, Senge,
   Heifetz, alongside an unwillingness to help harmful leadership become more
   efficient at it.

The through-line: money, buildings, compliance, and leadership culture are one
system viewed from different angles. Most consultants pick a lane. This practice
refuses to, and that refusal is the competitive position.

## Deliberately not offered

Leadership coaching · strategic planning retreats · curriculum development ·
board development training · marketing and communications

This list is published on the services page on purpose. The trap in this
practice has always been that the credentials (MA Global Leadership, Fuller;
DMin Leadership, Pacific School of Religion) pull the work toward leadership
coaching, which is the least enjoyable part of it. Naming the exclusions lets
unwanted inquiries self-select out before they become conversations that feel
obligatory.

The credentials still appear on the site, framed as the diagnostic instrument
behind the assessment rather than as a coaching offer. Organizational life cycle
theory tells you which failures are predictable at which stage. That is why the
assessment is good.

## The methodology

1. **Assess.** What is actually happening? Ninety days across finances,
   staffing, compliance, systems, facilities, governance. No prescriptions
   before understanding.
2. **Prioritize.** What has to be fixed first? A short, ordered, approved plan
   sequenced by risk and dependency.
3. **Execute.** Who does the work? We do. Not a report handed over for someone
   else to implement.
4. **Hand off.** What happens when we leave? Documented processes, trained
   staff, a clear exit. The goal of every engagement is to become unnecessary.

## How to write about this work

The category is hard to name because it is defined by what it is not: not
strategy, not vision, not coaching. The failure mode is describing it in the
same abstract register as strategy work ("operational effectiveness,"
"organizational health"), which makes it sound like strategy again.

The fix is to write in **artifacts** and **triggers**.

**Artifacts** are what exists at the end that did not before: a budget that
reconciles, books that close monthly, payroll that runs without you, a handbook
that matches actual practice, a filed 990, a current charitable registration, a
documented process, an onboarded successor.

**Triggers** are the moment someone picks up the phone: the bookkeeper quit,
the ED leaves in ninety days, the audit is in six weeks, they outgrew the
founder doing everything, compliance lapsed and nobody noticed, the nonprofit
status needs reinstating.

Nobody buys operations consulting. They buy relief from something specific that
is broken right now. Lead with the trigger, prove with the artifact.

One more line that does real work, because these buyers arrive carrying shame:

> None of that is a leadership failure. It is an infrastructure failure, and
> infrastructure can be rebuilt.

## Voice rules

- No em dashes. Rework the sentence rather than substituting a comma.
- Concrete over abstract. Name the deliverable, not the capability.
- Short declaratives. The register is competent and calm, not visionary.
- Say what is not offered as readily as what is.
